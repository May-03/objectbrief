> **Quick answer.** A swing top bottle can take a shrink band, but only a wide, tall one. A UK specialist sells a 70 x 70 mm clear PETG band with a vertical perforation for these bottles, rated for a widest point of 38 to 44 mm. The band has to slide over the stopper and the wire lever, then shrink down to a much narrower neck, which is why at least one US bottle supplier does not recommend bands on swing tops. Test the fit with a paper tube before you buy, heat from the bottom up, and consider a tab label over the stopper if the band will not sit cleanly.

## Why is a swing top awkward to band?

Look at how the closure is built. A packaging supplier describes three parts: a plug with a gasket, and a wire bail that works as a lever to clamp the plug down on the mouth of the bottle [1]. The wire is anchored in the glass a little way down the neck and stands proud of it on both sides.

That gives a band three problems at once.

- The widest point is not the neck. The band has to pass over the stopper and the wire, which are wider than the glass beneath them.

- The surface is not round. Wire loops and a lever stick out, so film stretches over bumps instead of lying against glass.

- The shrink is large. Once past the hardware, the band has to close down onto a neck that may be much narrower.

One more thing separates these bottles from screw-cap ones. On a screw cap, a tight band stops the cap turning. On a swing top, the band has to stop the lever lifting. A band that grips only the glass below the wire does nothing.

## Do suppliers recommend it or not?

They disagree, and the disagreement is useful.

Against. A US bottle supplier that sells both swing top bottles and shrink bands says plainly that its bands are not recommended for swing tops, nor for plastic bottles, tapered cork closures or some straight-sided jars, and that they are not intended to prevent leakage [2]. Its stock bands are short, 25 to 30 mm tall, and sized for ordinary caps [2]. On a swing top, a band like that is too narrow to pass the hardware or too short to cover it.

For. A UK specialist lists a band made for the job: 70 mm wide when laid flat and 70 mm tall, clear PETG, with a vertical perforation, for a widest diameter of 38 to 44 mm. The seller says these fit many flip and swing top drinks bottles and are applied with a hot air gun [3]. Some consumer bottle kits also come with "shrink wrap pieces" in the box alongside funnels and tags [4], which tells you home users are doing this, though the kit listings give no sizes.

The two positions are consistent once you notice the dimensions. Ordinary cap bands do not suit swing tops. A much larger band can.

## What size should you try?

Start from the UK listing, since it is the only swing-top-specific size the sources reviewed run to. A band 70 mm wide when flat has a circumference of 140 mm and opens to a circle about 44.5 mm across, which matches the seller's stated maximum of 44 mm [3]. At 70 mm tall, it is more than twice the height of a standard cap band.

Then measure your own bottle, because swing tops are not standardised in the way screw necks are. You need three numbers:

- The widest point across the closed stopper and wire, measured with calipers or by wrapping a strip of paper around it and halving the length.

- The diameter of the glass neck just below the wire anchor.

- The distance from the top of the stopper down to where you want the band to end.

The band's opened diameter must be larger than the first number. Its height must be at least the third. The second number tells you how hard the film has to work.

Here is the sum, using made-up figures for illustration. If the band opens to 44.5 mm and your neck below the wire is 28 mm, the film must shrink by about 37 percent to grip the glass, since 28 divided by 44.5 is 0.63. Film makers quote maximum shrink of 55 to 65 percent for PVC and 75 percent or more for PETG [5], so the figure is within reach of both. The margin is wider with PETG, and that may be why the specialist chose it. That last remark is an inference; the seller does not explain the choice. More on the two films in [PVC vs PETG shrink bands], and on the calculation in [shrink ratio and film direction](/blog/shrink-band-shrink-ratio-td-vs-md).

## How do you check the fit before buying?

The UK seller gives a tip that costs nothing: make a tube that is 70 mm wide when flattened and see whether it slides over your closed swing top [3]. Here is how to do it properly.

- Cut a strip of ordinary paper about 145 mm long and 70 mm tall.

- Tape the short ends together with a 5 mm overlap, so the loop is 140 mm around. Flattened, it should measure 70 mm.

- Close the bottle and lower the paper tube over the top.

- If it passes the stopper and the wire without tearing, the band will go on. If it jams, you need a wider band or a different seal.

- With the tube in place, mark where its bottom edge sits. That is roughly where the band will end before shrinking.

The same trick works for any size. Double the lay-flat width to get the loop length.

## How do you shrink it without a mess?

The seller's instruction is brief: slip the band over the bottle and heat it with a hot air gun until it shrinks tight [3]. The detail below is a suggestion based on how tall bands behave on other bottles, and is untested on swing tops.

- Close the bail fully and check the gasket is seated. A band will lock in whatever position the stopper is in.

- Lower the band so its top edge stands a few millimetres above the stopper. A packaging distributor's general rule is about 5 mm of overhang at each end of a closure [6].

- Heat the bottom of the band first, on the glass, turning the bottle. Once it grips the neck it will stop sliding.

- Work upward over the wire in short passes. Expect the film to bridge the gaps beside the wire; it will not hug every curve.

- Finish with a pass over the top edge so it folds in over the stopper.

- Let it cool, then try to lift the lever with a thumb. If the lever moves freely under the film, the band is not doing its job.

Keep the heat low and moving. The gasket on the stopper is rubber or silicone [1], and there is no reason to cook it. If the film splits where it crosses the wire, see [shrink band problems and fixes](/blog/shrink-band-problems-and-fixes).

## Which way should the perforation run?

The swing-top band the sources reviewed run to has a vertical perforation [3]. That suits this bottle. The buyer tears one line and the whole sleeve comes away, leaving bare glass and a closure that works as normal, which matters because people keep and refill these bottles. A band with a ring perforation would leave a collar of film trapped under the wire. If a seller offers a choice, ask for vertical. Our [shrink band perforation guide](/blog/shrink-band-perforation-guide) covers the options.

## What if the band will not sit cleanly?

Then use a seal designed for irregular closures. A label converter describes tab labels, a strip that runs over the closure and down the sides, as a common tamper-evident choice in food, wine and spirits [7]. On a swing top, a strip from the glass on one side, over the stopper, to the glass on the other side has to be torn before the lever can lift. It needs no heat and it can carry your name.

The same converter mentions a larger-scale option: a full-body shrink sleeve extended over the closure, with perforations that let the buyer tear off only the top section [7]. That is a custom printed product with minimum orders, not a bag of stock bands.

A sticker has limits of its own. US guidance for over-the-counter drugs sets out the principle for any band or wrapper: it must have to be cut or torn to open the container, and it must not be possible to work it off and put it back without visible damage [8]. That guidance does not apply to limoncello or syrup, but it is a sound test for any seal. A paper strip that peels cleanly in one piece fails it. Our comparison of [shrink bands vs tamper-evident stickers](/blog/shrink-bands-vs-tamper-evident-stickers) goes into which labels tear and which lift.

## A caution for fizzy and fermenting drinks

Swing tops are popular for kombucha and other live drinks, and one packaging distributor notes that the closure allows slight pressure release, which it considers useful during fermentation [9]. A band is thin film. It will not hold a stopper down against pressure, and nothing in the sources suggests it should be asked to. If your product is still fermenting in the bottle, sort out the fermentation and storage side first. Nothing here was research carbonation safety, and a band is not part of that answer.

## Should you band a swing top at all?

- Gifts and tasting-room sales: probably not needed. The buyer watched you hand it over.

- Shelf sales through a shop or market stall you do not staff: yes, some visible seal is sensible. Try the tall band first, a tab label second.

- Online orders: a seal shows the bottle arrived unopened, but it will not stop leaks. Pack for the journey as well.

- Anything you are told must be tamper-evident by a stockist: ask what they will accept before buying supplies.

On the legal side, the sources reviewed run to no general US federal rule requiring tamper-evident packaging on ordinary foods and drinks; see [are shrink bands required by law](/blog/are-shrink-bands-required-by-law). Nothing here was research alcohol rules, which vary and are a separate matter for limoncello and similar products.

## FAQ

### Will a wine shrink capsule fit a swing top?

Standard wine capsules are about 30 mm across, well under the 38 to 44 mm range of the swing-top band [3]. On those numbers, no.

### Can I use a hair dryer?

The seller specifies a hot air gun [3]. A band this large has a long way to shrink, so a dryer is likely to be slow. Try it on one before deciding.

### Does the band keep the stopper from popping in transit?

It may resist a knock to the lever. The sources reviewed run to no source that tests this, and one supplier says its bands are not meant to prevent leakage [2].

### Is the film safe near the drink?

The band sits outside the closure. If food contact matters to you, ask the seller for a written statement about the film.

### Do mini swing top bottles need a smaller band?

Almost certainly. Measure the widest point and use the paper tube.
