> **Quick answer.** Most Marasca and Dorica olive oil bottles have a 31.5 mm screw neck, and many of the caps sold for them already include a break-away tamper ring, so a shrink band adds little. A band or capsule earns its place when you close the bottle with a plain screw cap, a cork or a bar-top stopper. For a 31.5 mm cap, look for a band with a lay-flat width of about 52 to 55 mm, or a capsule of 33 mm diameter or more; 31 mm capsules suit corked necks. Match the height to your cap, which may be 24, 29 or 44 mm tall, and wipe oil off the neck first.

## What neck does an olive oil bottle have?

The square Marasca and the round Dorica are the two shapes most oil sellers recognise, and both are commonly made with a 31.5 mm screw neck. UK bottle suppliers list their 100, 250 and 500 ml Marasca bottles with a 31.5 mm neck and note that it accepts their range of 31.5 mm closures [1]. European distributors write the same finish as "PP 31.5" [2]. An Australian olive supplier describes a metal pourer cap as compatible with most 31.5 mm oil and vinegar bottles, naming Dorica, Marasca and round Dorica styles [3].

That is the common case, not a law of nature. Other finishes exist; one European maker's caps come in 28 mm as well [4]. Check the finish on your bottle's specification before you order anything that has to fit over it.

## Which closures already show tampering?

|Closure|Built-in tamper evidence?|Can you apply it by hand?|
|---|---|---|
|Plastic screw cap with break-away ring, with or without pourer|Yes. The ring breaks on first opening [5]|Yes [6]|
|Pre-threaded aluminium cap with tear-off band|Yes [4]|Sold for manual capping [4]|
|Unthreaded aluminium cap with tamper band|Yes, once formed onto the neck [4]|No. One seller states a capping machine is required [3]|
|Plain screw cap, no ring|No|Yes|
|Cork or bar-top stopper|No|Yes|

The first row is the one most small producers want. A UK closure seller describes its 31.5 mm plastic cap as having an integrated band that breaks away cleanly on first opening [5]. An Australian-made version with a pourer insert is sold specifically for hand application without a capping machine, with a stated cap height of 29.1 mm [6]. Bottle-and-cap bundles from UK suppliers ship with this kind of cap as standard [1].

If you already use one of these, you have your tamper signal. A band over it is not harmful, but it is a second layer the customer must remove, and it hides the ring that is doing the work.

## When is a band or capsule worth adding?

- You use plain caps. Nothing shows a first opening. A perforated band is the simplest fix.

- You cork the bottle. A cork can be pulled and pushed back. A capsule over the top is the traditional answer, and one supplier describes a shrink cap as sealing both the cork and the bottle [7].

- You sell through Amazon's warehouses. Their prep rules for liquids ask for a second seal on top of a tight cap. Our page on [shrink bands and the FBA double seal] covers what counts.

- You want the neck to carry colour or branding. A coloured capsule is a cheap way to tell an infused oil from a plain one on a shelf.

- A stockist asks for it. Some shops want a visible seal regardless of the cap.

One buyer who made infused olive oil reported that coloured shrink caps fitted their bottles well and shrank cleanly with a hair dryer [8]. It is a single review, but it shows the use is ordinary.

## Band or capsule: what is the difference?

A band is an open ring of film. A capsule has a closed top, like a small cup. Bands are sold by lay-flat width, the width of the flattened ring. Capsules are sold by diameter. The two numbers are not interchangeable, a point covered at length in [shrink capsules for wine bottles](/blog/shrink-capsules-for-wine-bottles).

Capsules for this neck size also come in two styles of their own. One supplier lists a 33.5 x 40 mm transparent capsule with an open top and a vertical perforation, and a 33 x 40 mm matte black capsule with a closed top and a horizontal tear strip [9]. The open-top version behaves like a tall band. The closed-top version hides the cap completely.

## What size fits a 31.5 mm cap?

For a band. A packaging distributor's formula is to measure the circumference, halve it and add 2 mm [10]. A 31.5 mm cap has a circumference of about 99 mm, so the sum gives 51.5 mm. Stock bands near that figure include a 55 x 28 mm band that one large seller rates for caps of 1 to 1 1/4 inches, which is 25 to 32 mm [11]. A 55 mm band opens to about 35 mm, so it slides over a 31.5 mm cap with room to spare and has to shrink by roughly 10 percent to grip. Measure your own cap, because knurled or flared caps run wider than the thread size suggests.

For a capsule. The capsule must be wider than the cap it covers. That is why the 33 and 33.5 mm capsules exist [9]. The same supplier also sells 31 x 40, 31 x 42 and 31 x 60 mm capsules, described as sealing a cork and bottle [7]. On the numbers alone, a 31 mm capsule will not pass over a 31.5 mm screw cap, so those read as sizes for corked or bar-top necks. That is an inference from the dimensions. The supplier does not say it in so many words, and its own advice is simply to make sure length and diameter are right [7].

For height. Caps for this neck are sold at 24 mm, 29.1 mm and 44 mm tall [4][6]. The distributor's guide suggests the band should extend about 5 mm past the closure at the top and bottom so that it grips the glass below [10].

|Your cap height|Band height to look for|What is sold|
|---|---|---|
|24 mm|About 34 mm|40 mm capsules; 28 mm bands will sit short [9][11]|
|29 mm|About 39 mm|40 to 42 mm capsules [7][9]|
|44 mm|About 54 mm|60 mm capsules [7]|

The middle column is arithmetic done here from the 5 mm rule. A 28 mm stock band on a 24 mm cap covers the cap and only a sliver of the neck. It will show tampering if it is perforated and tight, but it has less glass to hold on to. One supplier also notes that on its capsules the tear strip sits 18 mm from the top [7]. Check where that line falls against your cap: ideally at or just below the join between cap and glass.

## How do you apply it on an oily bottle?

Suppliers list the usual heat sources for capsules: a heat gun, boiling water or steam, or a capsule tool [7]. The steps below are ours and untested, adapted for oil.

- Fill, cap and tighten. Then wipe the neck and shoulder with a dry cloth, and again with a cloth dampened with a little warm soapy water if oil has run. Film will not sit evenly on a greasy surface.

- Let the glass dry completely.

- Slide the band or capsule on. For a capsule, press the top flat on the cap.

- Heat from the top down with a gun on low or a hair dryer, turning the bottle, for a few seconds. A hot-water dip suits capsules on corked bottles; keep it to the neck.

- Stand the bottle upright to cool, then try to turn the cap through the film. It should not move without tearing something.

Keep the heat brief and at the neck. Producers generally protect oil from heat and light, which is why dark glass is offered for it [3], and there is no reason to warm the body of the bottle. The sources reviewed run to no source measuring the effect of a few seconds of neck heat on oil, so no claim is made either way. If a band wrinkles or slips, see [shrink band problems and fixes](/blog/shrink-band-problems-and-fixes).

## Mistakes that are easy to make

- Ordering by thread size alone. A "31.5 mm" cap is 31.5 mm at the thread. The outside of the skirt, especially on a knurled plastic cap, can be wider. Measure the outside.

- Buying unthreaded aluminium caps for hand bottling. They are formed onto the glass by a machine [3]. Without one they will not grip.

- Banding over a tamper ring and calling it two seals. It is one seal shown twice. That may still be what a stockist wants, but be clear about why you are doing it.

- Choosing a band that stops at the bottom of the cap. If the film never touches glass, the cap and band can turn together.

- Covering the pour-date or lot mark. If you write a date on the neck or shoulder, keep the film clear of it.

## What to ask before you order

For closures, ask the seller for the neck finish the cap is made for, the outside diameter and height of the cap, whether it carries a break-away ring, what liner it has, and whether it can be applied by hand. For bands and capsules, ask for lay-flat width or diameter, height, film and thickness, the position of any perforation or tear strip, and whether the top is open or closed. Then buy the smallest pack and try five bottles. A supplier's own advice is the right note to end on: make sure the length and the diameter are right for your bottle [7].

## What does each option cost?

Enough to matter at volume, not at a market stall. A stock 55 x 28 mm PVC band was listed at $16 per thousand, under 2 US cents each [11]. A plain 31.5 mm plastic cap with a tamper ring was listed at £0.15 before VAT [5], and a gold aluminium pourer cap at $0.24 in an Australian store [12]. A 250 ml Marasca bottle with a tamper-evident pourer cap included was £1.04 for small quantities [1].

Those figures are in three currencies and cannot be added up, but the comparison is clear enough. Moving from a plain cap to a tamper-ring cap costs more per bottle than a band does. It also saves a step on every bottle and removes the heat gun from your bottling day. For a few hundred bottles a year, either route is affordable. Choose on labour and looks. ## Is a seal required on olive oil?

Not under the US federal tamper-evident rules reviewed here, which cover over-the-counter drugs and two narrow cosmetic types. The sources reviewed run to no general federal tamper-evident rule for ordinary foods; see [are shrink bands required by law](/blog/are-shrink-bands-required-by-law). Nothing here was research state rules, other countries, or the separate food-safety guidance that applies to oils infused with fresh ingredients such as garlic or herbs. If you make infused oils, ask your local food authority about those before you think about packaging.

## Which should you choose?

- Tamper-ring cap already on the bottle: skip the band, unless a stockist or Amazon asks for a second seal.

- Plain cap: a perforated band about 55 mm lay-flat, tall enough to reach the glass.

- Cork or bar top: a closed-top capsule sized to the neck, with a tear strip.

- Buying new closures anyway: a hand-applied tamper-ring cap is the tidier answer.

## FAQ

### Will a wine capsule fit an olive oil bottle?

Standard wine capsules are about 30 mm across. That is narrower than a 31.5 mm screw cap, so on the numbers it will not slide over one. It may suit a corked oil bottle with a slimmer neck. Measure first.

### Can I band over a pourer cap?

Yes, if the band clears the cap's widest point. The pourer insert sits inside the neck and does not change the outside size [1].

### Does the band stop leaks?

No. A liner inside the cap does that; one supplier describes an EPE liner as creating the seal between cap and neck [12]. The band only shows opening.

### Do bands work on vinegar bottles?

The same bottles and 31.5 mm closures are sold for vinegar and dressings [1], so the sizing here carries over.
