> **Quick answer.** A machine described as "double-sided" may apply two separate labels in one pass, but that phrase does not define where either label should land. Before comparing models, decide whether the two labels simply need to sit on opposite sides of a cylindrical bottle or must align to a bottle feature such as a seam, handle, molded panel, or existing mark. That distinction determines whether fixed opposing label heads are enough or the bottle needs controlled orientation.

This guide covers pressure-sensitive front and back labels on round bottles. It helps buyers define the package, state the relationship between the two labels, and test the complete setup before accepting a machine. It does not establish a universal speed or placement tolerance; those must be agreed for the actual bottle, labels, and line.

## Start with the Label Job, Not the Machine Name

"Front" and "back" can describe two different tasks. On a featureless cylinder, they may mean labels on opposing sides as the bottle travels between two applicators. If artwork must line up with a molded seam, grip, handle, logo, or code, the bottle needs a repeatable orientation datum. Merely installing two heads opposite each other does not prove that the package enters them at the right angle.

A supplier may offer a positioning sensor or indexing option, but ask what the system detects and what it controls: the bottle's angular position, label release timing, or both. Have the supplier show the marked bottle entering the machine and the finished front and rear labels in the agreed inspection view.

| Packaging need | Likely route to evaluate | Question to settle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One continuous label around the cylindrical body | Wraparound labeler | Can one roll cover the intended area and leave the seam where the artwork allows? |
| Two separate panels on opposite sides; no bottle feature to align | Twin-head / double-side labeler | How are the two label positions adjusted independently, and how is spacing held? |
| Two separate panels aligned to a seam, handle, molded feature, or mark | Positioning or indexing configuration | What datum is sensed, and what repeatability is demonstrated with the actual bottle? |

Round bottles are often handled by a wraparound system, while separate front/back labels are also possible. Current buyer guidance stresses checking bottle orientation and label spacing with samples; treat that as a route-selection prompt, not a rule that every round bottle needs the same machine.

## Define the Relationship Between Both Labels

Use one bottle drawing or marked sample as the reference. Name the viewing direction, mark the front datum, and dimension the front and rear label positions from that same reference. If the bottle has no physical front feature, say that explicitly and define the relationship relative to the conveyor or machine instead.

| Specify separately | Record for each format |
| --- | --- |
| Bottle | Diameter and height; taper or shoulder; base stability; empty or filled condition; material; seam, handle, molded feature, or other orientation datum. |
| Front label roll | Label width and length; gap or pitch; face and liner; roll direction and core; artwork leading edge; target height and lateral position. |
| Rear label roll | The same roll details, plus how its artwork and position relate to the front label. Do not assume its size, winding, or feed direction matches the front roll. |
| Line and changeover | Target accepted bottles per minute; conveyor direction; upstream/downstream equipment; format changes; roll changes; coding or barcode inspection needs. |

If the front and rear labels have different dimensions or artwork, request independent adjustments and written setup values for each head. A shared "double label spacing" adjustment may control the gap between labels around the circumference, but it does not by itself define height, centering, skew, or alignment to a bottle datum.

## Do Not Take a Headline Speed as the Acceptance Rate

A listing can show a speed range that reflects different configurations, bottle sizes, label sizes, or test conditions. Ask the supplier to state which exact configuration and label-count basis the quoted rate uses. Clarify whether "pieces per minute" means bottles with both labels correctly applied or individual label applications; for a two-label job, the finished-bottle rate is what matters.

The Alibaba listing discussed below contains several different model and specification entries on the same page. That is a reason to request one signed configuration sheet before purchase. Do not choose whichever number looks most attractive or assume that the broadest range applies to your bottle.

## Run a Two-Label Acceptance Test

Send production-intent bottles and both label rolls. Include the actual bottle condition at labeling and the smallest and largest formats you expect to run.

Mark the bottle datum and submit artwork or a placement drawing. State front and rear label height, centering, angular relationship or gap, allowed skew, and cosmetic rejection conditions.

Have the supplier run both heads together at the required finished-bottle rate. Capture the bottle at entry, between application points, and at discharge so you can see whether it rotates, slips, or changes orientation.

Inspect every defined acceptance point: front placement, rear placement, relationship between labels, wrinkles or bubbles, edge lift, missed labels, and bottle marks. Agree sample size and pass criteria before the trial; no generic percentage or tolerance fits every application.

Repeat after a normal stop/restart and at least one roll change or format change relevant to your operation. Record head settings, guides, sensor positions, roll direction, speed, sample photos, and the approved first-off check.

A successful sensor trigger or a neat single bottle is not enough. Accept the configuration only when both labels meet the agreed criteria together at the required rate, including the restart or changeover conditions that matter in production.

## A Short RFQ Note

We need two separate self-adhesive labels on a round bottle. Bottle diameter/height: [ ]. Bottle condition during labeling: [ ]. Front datum or orientation feature: [ ]. Front label size and roll direction: [ ]. Rear label size and roll direction: [ ]. Required label height and front-to-rear relationship: [ ]. Required accepted output: [ ] finished bottles/minute with both labels applied. Please confirm the exact machine model, included positioning components, applicable bottle and label ranges, and the configuration used to quote the speed. Before shipment, run both labels on our samples and provide the recorded speed, settings, and agreed placement results.
