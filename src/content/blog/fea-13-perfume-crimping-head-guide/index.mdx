> **Quick answer.** Do not order a perfume crimper from the "13 mm" label alone. First confirm the bottle's documented FEA 13 neck finish, the pump supplier's matching ferrule and collar, and the exact crimp-head configuration offered with the machine. The Alibaba listing selected for this guide explicitly lists a "machine + FEA13 (13mm neck) crimp + collar head" option, but that listing does not prove that every bottle and pump sold as FEA 13 will crimp correctly with it. Send the supplier the bottle drawing, pump and ferrule specification, and a physical sample where possible. Ask for a trial using that exact set, then check collar formation, pump movement, bottle damage, and leakage against your own acceptance procedure before production.

The useful purchase decision is therefore a matched set, not a machine model: bottle finish + pump/ferrule + crimp head + bottle support. If one component changes, repeat the fit and seal check.

## What "FEA 13" Tells You and What It Does Not

FEA 13 is a finish designation used for a crimp-style fragrance bottle and its compatible pump family. It is a useful starting point for sourcing, not a complete interchangeability guarantee. Actual compatibility depends on the finish geometry, the pump ferrule and gasket, the way the ferrule is formed, and the tooling that contacts it. CETIE's perfume bottling guide treats correct crimping as a package-specific operation on an FEA glass finish and points to the applicable finish dimensions and good-practice checks. It does not say that any tool marked "13" is suitable for every bottle and pump. [2]

The number on a machine option can also be shorthand chosen by a seller. In the candidate listing, the FEA13 option is described as a 13 mm neck crimp collar head. Treat that as the supplier's offered configuration; confirm what dimension the head is designed around and which pump ferrule it is intended to form. Do not infer the dimension from a product photo or assume that a "13 mm" head opening equals the bottle's nominal finish diameter.

This matters most on very small flacons. A pump can sit unevenly, the collar can wrinkle or remain loose, and excess force can stress glass. A lever tool makes the operator the source of force, so consistent positioning and a defined end point matter as much as nominal head size.

## Check the Whole Package Before Choosing the Head

Before requesting a quote, collect these details from the bottle and pump suppliers. Keep their drawings or written specifications together so the crimper supplier sees the same package you intend to run.

| Item to confirm | What to obtain |
| --- | --- |
| Bottle finish | The bottle drawing or supplier confirmation that identifies the FEA 13 finish and its critical dimensions. |
| Pump and ferrule | The pump model, ferrule material and dimensions, gasket arrangement, and confirmation that it is intended for the bottle finish. |
| Crimp head | The exact head included in the quote, its stated finish/ferrule compatibility, and whether a head change is required for other sizes. |
| Bottle support | How the tool locates and supports the specific bottle body and height without rocking or loading a fragile shoulder. |
| Acceptance checks | The supplier's recommended setup procedure and an agreed sample test for collar formation, pump retention, glass condition, and leakage. |

A useful supplier question is: "Please confirm in writing that the quoted FEA 13 head is designed for this bottle finish and this pump ferrule. Can you crimp samples using our actual bottle and pump, and return close-up photos plus the test method and results?" This is more actionable than asking whether the machine is simply "for perfume bottles."

## Run a Controlled First Sample

Use a short, documented trial before committing the machine to production. The trial should establish whether the head, package, and operator method work together; it should not be treated as proof that the package will remain leak-free in every transport or storage condition.

- **Inspect the incoming parts.** Check the neck and bead for visible chips, cracks, contamination, or inconsistent finish. Confirm the pump ferrule is not dented and that the pump and gasket are the specified components.
- **Set the tool for the actual bottle.** Install the quoted FEA 13 head and adjust the bottle support so the bottle stands squarely beneath it. Follow the supplier's setup instructions; do not add force to compensate for a tilted bottle or the wrong head.
- **Crimp a small trial group under one recorded setup.** Keep the bottle, pump lot, head, adjustment, and operator method constant. Record any head movement, bottle rotation, unusual lever effort, or change in collar appearance.
- **Inspect each finished sample.** Look for a visibly even ferrule around the finish, a pump that does not rock or lift under the agreed gentle check, and no cracked glass, crushed collar, or distorted pump. Do not use an improvised force value as a pass/fail limit.
- **Run the agreed leak check.** Use the fragrance/package owner's defined method and acceptance criteria. A visual crimp inspection alone cannot establish leak integrity. Record the test conditions and retain representative passing and failing samples.
- **Freeze the approved setup.** Record the part identifiers, head designation, support/height position, adjustment, inspection method, leak test, results, and date. Repeat qualification when the bottle finish, pump/ferrule, head, or other critical setup changes.

A 2024 revision of CETIE's public bottling-guide listing describes five good-practice rules for crimping an aluminium ferrule on an FEA glass finish. The guide is based on EN 14854 and CETIE GME 40.05, and specifically describes tool crimping rather than rotating-head rolling. If your supplier uses a different crimping mechanism, ask for the corresponding procedure instead of transferring a tool-crimp instruction to another process.

## Diagnose the Failure Before Increasing Force

- **The pump sits crooked.** Check whether the bottle is vertical in its support and whether the pump is centered before the lever stroke. Confirm that the head is the correct design and is not worn or obstructed.
- **The collar looks uneven or loose.** Compare the pump ferrule specification with the quoted head and supplier setup instructions. Inspect the finish and sample another pump; do not assume a harder press will correct a mismatch.
- **The glass chips or cracks.** Stop the trial. Check for pre-existing damage, poor support, off-axis loading, and an incompatible or damaged head. Have the machine supplier review the sample and setup before continuing.
- **The crimp looks sound but the package leaks.** Keep the failed sample and check pump, gasket, finish, and test method as one system. A tighter crimp is not a universal remedy; it may deform the ferrule or damage the finish.

A small experimental study on a lever-type perfume sprayer crimper evaluated the tool's ability to prevent leakage across bottle heights. It supports the practical point that the press mechanism and bottle geometry must be assessed together, but it does not establish a universal force, head dimension, or acceptance limit for commercial FEA 13 packages. Set those limits with the component and equipment suppliers and the package owner. [1]

## Choose a Manual Tool When the Batch Size Fits the Work

A manual crimper can suit sampling, pilot batches, and low-throughput filling where the operator can position each bottle and inspect each result. It is less attractive when output targets, operator fatigue, or repeated changeovers make a hand-operated cycle difficult to keep consistent. In either case, ask how quickly heads can be changed, which spare parts are supplied, how the bottle support adjusts, and whether the supplier will demonstrate the exact package before shipment.

## Buyer Approval Checklist

- Bottle drawing or written FEA 13 finish confirmation is available.
- Pump supplier confirms the pump and ferrule match that bottle finish.
- The crimp-head designation and included configuration appear in the quote.
- The supplier has reviewed the actual bottle and pump or agreed to a sample run.
- The support keeps the bottle vertical and stable through the stroke.
- Inspection and leak acceptance criteria are written and package-specific.
- The approved head, setup, component identifiers, and results are recorded.

Approve the machine only when the exact bottle, pump, and head have passed the agreed trial. A label such as "FEA13" narrows the search; the matched sample run establishes whether this configuration is ready for your work.
