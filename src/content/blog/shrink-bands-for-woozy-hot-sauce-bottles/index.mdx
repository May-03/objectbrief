> **Quick answer.** For a 5 oz woozy bottle with a 24-414 cap, sellers pair bands of about 44 to 45 mm lay-flat width, and the height is a choice: 25 mm covers the cap seam only, while 46 to 52 mm runs down the neck. One bulk listing rates a 45 x 52 mm band for caps of 3/4 to 1 inch (19 to 26 mm), which leaves about 10 percent spare at the top of that range. A 10 oz bottle takes a wider band, such as 65 x 60 mm for lids of 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches. The thread number is not the cap width, so measure the capped bottle. Make sure the neck has a bead, because a band on a straight neck can slide off.

## What makes a woozy bottle different

A woozy is the classic hot sauce bottle: a round body, a long slim neck and a small opening. Retailers describe it that way, and TricorBraun says the style is meant for hot sauce, dressings, infused oil and barbecue sauce. The cap on a 5 oz glass woozy is usually a 24-414 continuous-thread closure, and a plastic insert called an orifice reducer sits in the neck so the sauce comes out by the drop.

That layout affects the band in two ways. The reducer is inside the neck, so the band has nothing to do with it; the band only goes around the cap and, if you choose a taller one, part of the neck. And the cap is small and the neck is long, which makes the band’s height a design decision and not just a sizing detail.

One supplier, Specialty Bottle, lists its 5 oz woozy at 1.75 inches wide and 6.8 inches tall with a 24/414 black cap. TricorBraun’s listings for 5 oz woozy bottles give a total height of about 6.5 to 6.6 inches and a base diameter of 1.65 inches for the plastic version and 1.86 inches for one glass version. The bodies differ, but all of them rely on the same 24 mm cap family, which is why one band size is often sold for the lot.

## Read the finish number correctly

Three finishes show up on 5 oz woozy bottles in the listings reviewed for this guide. Uline, Kaufman Container and Webstaurant sell the glass bottle as 24-414 (Uline writes 24/414), TricorBraun lists a plastic version with a 24-410 finish, and TricorBraun’s glass sauce bottle is described as 24-490 / 24-414, with a flip-top dispensing closure available for the 24-490 option. The first number is 24 mm in every case. The second describes the thread style.

None of these numbers is the outside width of the cap. A cap with a ribbed side is wider than its thread, and a flip-top is a different shape from a plain screw cap. Cary Company’s guide to matching a band says the first number in a band size must match the diameter of the cap and the second number should exceed the bottle’s diameter slightly, which is the same lay-flat idea used throughout this series. Take a caliper to your actual capped bottle, not a catalog entry.

## What sellers actually pair with a 5 oz woozy

Specialty Bottle publishes its own match for its 5 oz woozy: a band coded SB44X25, which reads as 44 x 25 mm, and a black band coded SB45X46B, which reads as 45 x 46 mm. A bulk-pack seller lists 45 x 52 mm bands, in clear, red, black, white and green, as made for hot sauce and woozy bottles and rated for 3/4 to 1 inch (19 to 26 mm) caps. The 45 x 52 row is the one to study. A 45 mm band opens to about 28.6 mm, and the top of the seller’s cap range is 26 mm, so a cap at the upper end has roughly ten percent to spare. That is in the 4 to 15 percent window that earlier articles found typical. At the bottom of the range, a 19 mm object would leave the band very loose, and no source was found that says how much a band of this film will shrink, so treat the range as a ceiling and not a promise.

A buyer review on the 45 x 52 listing makes the point from the other side: the band fits only a narrow bottle top, and a few millimetres wider would have suited the reviewer’s cap. Another reviewer on the same bulk pack said that, because of the cap’s shape, the band gave the cap a hug but the cap could still be unscrewed with the band on. Both are single reviews. They are a reminder that the listing’s range describes the diameter, not the cap’s profile.

## Choosing the height: cap seam or neck

Specialty Bottle’s two matches are 25 mm and 46 mm tall. The bulk pack is 52 mm. That spread is the real decision. A 25 mm band covers the cap skirt and the seam where the cap meets the neck, which is what a tamper band needs to do. A 46 to 52 mm band runs further down the neck.

A taller band is also a styling choice. In a forum thread on bands for 5 oz woozy bottles, one maker said he prefers a band that hides the fill line, because shoppers look for the fullest bottle, and he wrote that 50 mm is a minimum on his bottles and he thinks a short band that leaves the fill line visible looks bad. That is one seller’s preference, not a standard. It does raise a fair question: if your fill level is not consistent from bottle to bottle, a taller opaque band covers the variation, but it does not fix it, and it adds film cost.

The practical test is to fill ten bottles to your normal level, cap them, apply each band height you are considering, and look at the result from the front of a shelf. Choose the shorter band if the look is fine, since a shorter band uses less film and shrinks more evenly.

## The neck bead decides whether the band stays on

A band holds because it shrinks under a bead or lip on the neck. TricorBraun’s page for a 45 x 25 mm perforated band warns that the bottle selected must have a neck bead to hold the band in place. A reviewer on the bulk listing says it plainly: if the bottle is straight-sided, the band slides off because there is nothing to snag.

Woozy necks are long and slim, and not every maker moulds the same bead. Look at the neck of the bottle you actually buy, below the cap, and run a fingernail around it. If you feel a ridge and the cap sits just above it, the band has something to grip. If the neck is smooth, a wider band that reaches the shoulder may help, or you may need a different bottle. Do not guess from the thread number.

## Moving up to 10 oz

The next size up needs a wider band. A 65 x 60 mm black band is listed for bottle lids of 1 1/4 to 1 1/2 inches, and a buyer says it works on their 10 oz hot sauce bottles. TricorBraun’s hot sauce range covers 5, 10, 12 and 20 oz bottles across 24-490, 24-414, 38-400 and 28-400 finishes, so a 10 oz bottle may carry any of several closures. This guide could not tie a specific finish to a 10 oz band, so measure the cap and compare it with the table above.

The same arithmetic holds. A 65 mm band opens to about 41 mm. For a 38 mm cap the minimum width is about 60 mm, which leaves roughly nine percent spare, and a narrower cap leaves more. If your cap sits between sizes, take the band whose range clearly contains it rather than the one it nearly fits.

## Colour, film and how to shrink them

The bulk-pack seller offers clear, red, black, white and green, and Specialty Bottle sells a black version to match black caps. Colour is a branding decision, but it interacts with the fill line as discussed above, and with the cap: a red cap under a clear band shows the cap colour, while a black band hides it. The forum maker above preferred a non-clear band for that reason.

The listings describe the film as 2-mil PVC, which is about 0.05 mm, and one reviewer notes that this thin film can be hard to shrink consistently. The seller’s instructions say to use a heat gun or a heat tunnel. One buyer reported using a pot of boiling water, dipping the bottle until the band shrank. treat that as a single user’s workaround and test it on a sample first, since hot water and glass need care and a label can be damaged.

Sauce makers have a particular problem: residue. A drip on the cap or neck can leave a film under the band, which makes the shrink uneven and the surface sticky. Wipe the cap and neck dry before you apply a band. This is common practice rather than a sourced rule.

## Plastic and glass bottles do not behave the same

Both materials are sold as 5 oz woozies. Uline lists the plastic version as lightweight, shatterproof PET with a ribbed polypropylene cap and foam liner, and the glass version with a black or red polypropylene cap. The caps are in the same family, but the bottles squeeze differently. A PET bottle flexes when you grip it during heating and capping, so a band applied while the cap is still being torqued can end up unevenly seated.

Heat is the other difference. A heat gun held too long in one spot can soften a thin PET shoulder, while glass will not. No source was found that gives a safe temperature or time for PET woozy bottles, so keep the gun moving, test on empty bottles and stop at the first sign of distortion. If you sell both versions, record the band size and the method for each separately.

## Mistakes that cost sauce makers money

The first is ordering by bottle size. A listing that says “5 oz bottles” describes the bottle, and as the table shows, one band can be rated for a 19 to 26 mm cap while another is meant for a cap above 32 mm. The second is ordering by thread alone. A 24-414 label tells you the neck, not whether the cap is smooth, ribbed or a flip-top.

The third is buying a large bulk pack before testing. One of the listings above is a pack of 10,000, which is a poor way to learn that the band is a few millimetres too narrow, as one buyer reported. Buy a sample pack first, even if the per-band cost is higher. The fourth is skipping the perforation check; a band the customer cannot tear cleanly becomes a complaint about the sauce when it is really a complaint about the packaging, and our perforation guide covers the tear designs.

The fifth is forgetting the label. The band, the cap colour and the label are seen together on a shelf. Put a finished, labelled bottle in front of someone and ask what they notice first. If it is the band, the colour or height needs another look.

## A ten-bottle test

Order a sample pack of two band sizes and two heights. Apply them to ten finished bottles using the caps and the heat method you will use at volume. Check that the band goes on without forcing, shrinks without wrinkles on the neck, does not catch the label, and tears at the perforation without twisting the cap.

Then do the pull test: try to slide the band off a sealed bottle without tearing it, and try to unscrew the cap with the band on. If the cap turns freely inside the band, the band is acting as decoration, which is what the reviewer above found. Keep a record of the band supplier, size and height that passed, and measure three bottles from each new lot of glass before you reorder.

## FAQ

What size shrink band fits a 5 oz hot sauce bottle? Sellers pair bands of about 44 to 45 mm wide for 24-414 caps, in heights from 25 mm for the cap seam to about 52 mm for the neck. Measure your capped bottle and check the seller’s stated cap range.

Does the band cover the dripper insert? No. The orifice reducer sits inside the neck. The band goes around the cap.

Will a band fit a flip-top cap? No source was found on this. A flip-top is a different shape from a screw cap, so ask the seller for a cap-width range and test.

Why does my band slide off? The most likely cause in the sources reviewed is a straight neck with no bead. Check the neck, and try a wider or taller band if the bottle has a shoulder.

Do food sauces need a tamper band by law? This guide did not research it, and the answer depends on the product and the market. Check the rules for where you sell.
