> **Quick answer.** Small production runs still need repeatable closure checks. A cap that feels "about right" can vary with the operator, bottle finish, liner, cap material, and time after application. A manual digital torque tester gives a small team a repeatable way to sample threaded packages, log removal torque, and investigate shifts between lots. The useful result is not a universal pass number. It is a measurement tied to a defined package and a consistent test procedure.

## What This Test Measures

For a continuous-thread (screw-on) closure, removal torque is the rotational force needed to begin turning the cap off the container. Application torque is the torque used when the cap is first tightened. The readings are related, but they are not interchangeable: the removal result depends on the closure system and on what happened after capping, including elapsed time and storage conditions.

ASTM D2063/D2063M-24 describes a non-automated manual method for measuring torque retention in packages with continuous-thread closures. ASTM D3474-23 covers calibration and use of torque meters in packaging applications. Together, these references help define a repeatable measurement; they do not establish one acceptable torque value for every bottle and cap combination.

This guide is for small-batch sampling of threaded bottle and jar closures. It is not a test method for snap, lug, child-resistant, or other special closure systems unless the selected procedure and equipment are specifically suitable for them.

## Build a Repeatable Sampling Routine

**1. Define the package and the question.** Record the bottle material and finish, cap style and material, liner or insert, product or fill condition, and the capping process. Decide whether the check is meant to compare lots, investigate a capping adjustment, or study torque retention after storage. The test objective determines when samples are measured and which conditions must stay constant.

**2. Fix the conditioning and elapsed time.** Removal torque can change after application. Set a consistent interval between capping and measurement, and document any storage temperature or conditioning required by your procedure. If you are studying retention over time, use separate, matched samples for each time point; opening a closure changes that sample. ASTM D2063/D2063M-24 emphasizes predetermined environmental conditions and matching the container/closure system when results are compared.

**3. Secure and align the container.** Center the bottle or jar in the fixture and clamp it firmly enough to prevent rotation without crushing or deforming it. Keep the closure axis aligned with the tester's measurement axis. Confirm that the fixture accepts the container diameter and shape; a tester's advertised capacity is not proof that its included jaws hold your package securely.

**4. Capture the same reading every time.** Follow the tester's instructions for zeroing, direction, and peak capture. For a removal check, turn the closure in the specified direction at a consistent manual pace and record the peak breakaway/removal reading used by your procedure. Do not mix peak and running torque, or readings from different units, operators, conditioning intervals, or fixtures in one comparison unless your method allows it.

**5. Record enough to reproduce the result.** A useful log includes package/closure identification, lot, tester ID and calibration status, sample number, date, operator, conditioning and elapsed time, units, individual readings, and any observed slippage or damage. Keep individual values as well as a summary; averages alone can conceal an isolated outlier or a wide spread.

## How to Select a Manual Torque Tester

Start with your package and expected operating range, then compare the instrument to the job. Ask the supplier for written confirmation of these points before paying:

- **Measuring range and resolution** for the exact model being quoted. Choose a range that covers your expected readings with useful resolution; do not infer it from a product title or a neighboring model number.
- **Accuracy statement** and how it is expressed (for example, percent of reading or full scale), plus the calibration method and what certificate or traceability document is supplied.
- **Included bottle/cap fixture dimensions**, clamping range, compatible bottle shapes, and whether inserts or special jaws are extra.
- **Peak-hold behavior**, selectable units, data export or print options, and whether the device measures removal torque only or both application and removal.
- **Power, software, consumables**, delivery scope, warranty, lead time, and service/calibration support in your country.

Also ask for a demonstration or sample test using your actual bottle and closure. A fixture that grips without slipping and a range that suits your expected torque matter more than a long feature list. For repeated measurement, specify how you will verify the instrument between formal calibrations and how often calibration is required under your quality system.

## Avoid These Interpretation Errors

- Do not use a single generic torque target for all cap sizes and materials. Set limits from package-specific validation, closure/capping guidance, and your own quality requirements.
- Do not treat removal torque as proof of leak integrity. A torque reading describes rotational resistance; use a separate, appropriate integrity test when leak performance must be demonstrated.
- Do not compare results gathered at different elapsed times after capping or under different storage conditions without accounting for those differences.
- Do not assume a digital display makes the method repeatable by itself. Alignment, clamping, operator technique, range selection, and calibration all affect the usefulness of the reading.

## A Concise RFQ Checklist

- Bottle and cap drawings/photos, dimensions, materials, and closure style
- Expected torque interval and preferred unit, based on your own package data
- Exact tester model, range, resolution, accuracy basis, and included fixture
- Calibration certificate example, calibration/verification procedure, and service options
- Peak capture and data output requirements
- Price, sample/demo availability, shipping, warranty, lead time, and complete delivery scope

A good purchase decision starts with the package, not the catalog. Once your procedure defines the closure, timing, fixture, and target range, a compact manual tester can support dependable lot sampling without pretending to replace package validation or leak testing.
