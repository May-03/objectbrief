> **Quick answer.** Choose the trigger sprayer that matches the full neck finish printed on the bottle drawing: 28/400 for a 28/400 finish, or 28/410 for a 28/410 finish. The shared "28" is only one part of the designation. The "400" and "410" identify different finish specifications, and a matching nominal diameter alone does not prove a secure, repeatable fit. ISBT publishes separate voluntary finish guidelines for SP-400 and SP-410, which is why a buyer should confirm the complete code rather than order a generic "28 mm" head. [E01]

There can be product-specific exceptions. A retailer, for example, confirms that one named 28/410 sprayer fits one of its 28/400 Boston-round bottles. That answer applies to that exact pairing; it does not establish that all 28/410 sprayers fit all 28/400 bottles. [E03] Ask the supplier to confirm the exact sprayer model against your bottle drawing or sample, then approve a filled sample before a production order.

## Why the Last Three Digits Matter

A neck-finish code is a specification for the bottle opening and the closure that mates with it. In a code such as 28/410, the first number identifies the nominal finish size; the suffix identifies the finish series. ISBT's standards resource lists SP-400 and SP-410 as separate voluntary guidelines, with separate drawing numbers and size ranges. It does not treat the 400 and 410 suffixes as interchangeable labels for the same finish. [E01]

The bottle and sprayer also have physical dimensions beyond a nominal diameter. A technical sheet from Drug Plastics defines the T dimension as the outside diameter of the thread, and separately identifies the E, I, H, and S dimensions for the neck finish. Those are useful reminders that a package interface includes the thread and the surfaces around it, not just a single outside measurement. [E02] The actual controlling specification should come from the bottle supplier's drawing or written finish confirmation.

That distinction is easy to miss on a replacement order. A spray head can look close to the original, start onto the neck, or appear to tighten after a partial turn while still failing to seat as intended. The code on a listing may also describe one selectable configuration among several. A photo, the word "28 mm," or the fact that two components seem to engage by hand is not a substitute for a confirmed finish match.

## What Buyers Are Actually Trying to Solve

A replacement-parts question on Reddit describes a common situation: the user has 28/410 HDPE bottles but can find replacement sprayers labeled 28/400, and asks whether the materials might flex enough to make them fit. That post is useful evidence of the buyer's uncertainty, not a technical test result. [E04] The correct next move is not to rely on plastic flexibility. It is to identify the bottle finish and confirm the exact replacement head.

This matters most when a small brand has already bought bottles, has a trigger that has failed, or needs a new sprayer source without changing the container. In those cases, changing the head can be less disruptive than sourcing a new bottle — but only if the new component mates with the actual finish and performs with the product. A one-off fit at a workbench does not settle sealing, shipment, or repeated-use performance.

## 28/400 and 28/410 at a Glance

Both specifications begin with 28, so a quick visual comparison can make them seem equivalent. The key difference for procurement is the finish series recorded after the slash. Treat each code as its own bottle-and-closure interface unless the sprayer manufacturer confirms a specific cross-fit and supplies a sample for evaluation.

If a product page shows "28/400, 28/410" as options, read that as a range of selectable configurations until the supplier says otherwise. It may mean the seller offers separate versions of the sprayer. It does not, by itself, show that one physical sprayer is designed to mate with both finishes. Ask for the selected model or closure part number and have the finish shown on the quotation.

## Start with the Bottle Drawing, Not a Guess

Find the bottle SKU and the latest drawing revision. Look for the full neck-finish notation, such as 28/400 or 28/410, and confirm that it applies to the exact bottle you plan to fill. If the drawing only says "28 mm," request the full finish from the bottle supplier. Do not infer it from capacity, bottle height, resin, shoulder shape, or an online photograph; none of those identifies the complete finish by itself.

If there is no drawing, ask the bottle supplier to identify the finish in writing or send a sample to the sprayer supplier. A physical sample can resolve ambiguity, but keep the supplier's written confirmation with the sample record. This helps distinguish the actual bottle from a visually similar bottle, a supplier substitution, or a later mold revision.

Next, tell the sprayer supplier whether you need 28/400 or 28/410 and provide the bottle SKU or sample. Ask whether the quoted head is supplied in that exact finish, whether the listing contains separate finish variants, and which model number will appear on the quotation and production order. If your bottle source and sprayer source use different finish terminology, ask them to reconcile it on the drawing before you approve samples.

## When a Supplier Says One Model Fits Both

Some catalogs list a sprayer family for both 28/400 and 28/410. Some product pages offer a size selector with several finish options. Those statements can be useful sourcing leads, but the supplier needs to clarify what "both" means for the exact SKU you will buy. It may refer to separate closure configurations under one listing, rather than one unchanged sprayer head that fits both finishes.

Ask the supplier to identify the configuration that will ship for each finish and whether any adapter, gasket, closure change, or other component differs. Do not assume the design from a photograph or ask a line operator to force the head onto a neck. The exact physical configuration should be recorded on the sample label and quote.

A retailer's answer about a named 28/410 sprayer and a named 28/400 bottle shows why universal rules can be misleading: a specific cross-fit may be supported by a seller's own product check. [E03] The buyer should preserve that specificity. Ask for confirmation against your bottle SKU and evaluate that pair. Do not generalize the seller's answer to another bottle, sprayer, or production revision.

## Run a Sample Fit Check Before Ordering

A useful sample check answers two different questions: does the sprayer engage and seat correctly on the bottle, and does the assembled package work under the conditions that matter to the product? Record the bottle SKU and drawing revision, sprayer model and finish, sample lot if available, and the date of the evaluation.

Begin with a clean, undamaged bottle and an identified sprayer sample. Inspect the neck and closure for visible damage or contamination. Start the sprayer by hand without forcing it. Confirm that it advances normally, reaches the intended seated position, and does not cross-thread, rock, or remain visibly askew. Compare the closure position with a known-good package if one exists. If resistance or seating looks wrong, stop and ask the supplier to review the components; do not use extra force to make a mismatch appear acceptable.

Once the dry fit is acceptable, test the complete assembly with the intended liquid and the selected tube and gasket. Fill and close samples using the planned process. Check for leakage or loosening during the handling, storage, orientation, and transport conditions relevant to your product. Operate the sprayer enough to confirm that it primes and delivers as required for the intended task. Define your own sample quantity, method, and pass criteria before starting; there is no universal sample count or leakage threshold that can be inferred from the finish code alone.

If your product is sensitive to orientation, temperature, storage time, or shipment vibration, include those conditions in the test plan. A quick hand fit only screens the mechanical interface. It does not establish long-term sealing, formula compatibility, dose, spray pattern, or transport performance. Record observations separately so a good result in one area does not hide a failure in another.

## A Correct Finish Is Only the First Compatibility Check

Matching 28/400 or 28/410 does not prove that the sprayer is right for the entire package. The dip tube must reach the intended pickup area without being too long for the bottle. The gasket and other wetted materials must suit the formula. The pump output and nozzle pattern must meet the user's task. These are separate qualification questions, not extra meanings hidden inside the neck-finish code.

Keep the checks in a practical order. First confirm the finish so the sprayer can be assembled correctly. Then evaluate the tube, seal, liquid contact, output, spray behavior, and user operation on the exact bottle-and-formula combination. Articles on tube length and output per stroke cover those measurements in detail; here, the boundary is that neither result can rescue an incorrectly specified neck finish.

## Put the Decision in the RFQ

A precise request for quotation reduces ambiguity when listings use several finish options. Send the bottle drawing or a physical sample and write the required finish explicitly. For example:

"Please quote trigger sprayer model [model] for bottle [SKU], drawing revision [revision], with a [28/400 or 28/410] neck finish. Confirm whether the listing's finish choices are separate configurations or whether the quoted assembly is stated to fit both. Identify the closure configuration and gasket, and confirm the selected sprayer against our drawing/sample. Please provide samples for fit, leak, and dispensing evaluation with our product or agreed test liquid. State the current MOQ and sample terms in the quotation."

If the supplier claims one SKU fits both finishes, request the written basis for that claim and samples of the exact configuration intended for your bottle. If they offer two variants, ask them to name and label each one separately. Keep commercial terms current in the quote; do not rely on an old listing screenshot for MOQ, lead time, or price.

## Common Mistakes That Lead to a Wrong Replacement

- Ordering by "28 mm" alone is the most direct error. The shared number does not identify the finish series.
- Ordering by bottle capacity is another shortcut: two bottles of equal capacity can have different neck finishes.
- Copying a supplier listing title without selecting the correct variant can also lead to a mismatch, especially when one page groups several closures together.

Do not assume that a head is correct because it begins to thread. A partial engagement can feel convincing while the assembly remains wrong. Do not use pliers, excess torque, heat, or manual reshaping to force the fit; those actions can damage the bottle or closure and obscure the original mismatch. Do not accept "it fits" as a complete qualification if the supplier has not identified the bottle and sprayer model involved.

Finally, do not use one successful sample to approve unrecorded substitutions. If the bottle, closure, gasket, sprayer model, or drawing changes, review whether the approved fit and package tests still apply. A supplier's product family name is not a substitute for configuration control.

## Questions Buyers Often Ask

**Can I use any 28 mm trigger sprayer on a 28 mm bottle?** No. Ask for the complete neck finish, including the suffix, and match the sprayer configuration to that code. "28 mm" by itself is incomplete.

**Can a 28/410 sprayer ever fit a 28/400 bottle?** A seller may confirm a specific sprayer-and-bottle pairing. Treat that as a product-specific exception and test the exact assembly; do not apply it to every 28/410 sprayer or 28/400 bottle. [E03]

**What if my bottle is marked only 28 mm?** Ask the bottle supplier for the full finish designation and drawing. If that information is unavailable, send an identified physical sample to the sprayer supplier and get the proposed finish and fit confirmation in writing before approving a sample order.

**If a sprayer listing offers 28/400 and 28/410, is the product universal?** Not necessarily. The page may represent two variants under one listing. Ask which exact configuration will ship and record the selected model and finish on the quote.

**Does the correct finish guarantee no leaks?** No. It confirms only the intended finish match. The complete assembly still needs evaluation with the actual bottle, gasket, formula, fill, closure process, and relevant handling conditions.
