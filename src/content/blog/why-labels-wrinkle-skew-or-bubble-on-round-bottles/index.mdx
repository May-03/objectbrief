> **Quick answer.** When a label lands crooked, wrinkles, or traps bubbles, changing the machine speed is rarely a good first move. Find where the defect begins: at label release, first contact with the bottle, the wrap or wipe step, or after the container leaves the machine. Then change one condition at a time and keep a sample of each result.

This guide is for pressure-sensitive labels applied to round bottles and jars. It helps separate feed and timing faults from bottle handling, label construction, and surface problems. It does not set a universal pressure, speed, or placement tolerance. Those depend on the actual label, bottle, machine, and finished-package standard. Quadrel's troubleshooting guide likewise treats label defects as interactions among material, product handling, sensors, web tension, peel geometry, and settings; it is useful as a diagnostic reference, while specific machine adjustments must follow that machine's manual.

## Start with the Defect Pattern

| What you see | First place to inspect | A useful first check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Every bottle is shifted in the same direction | Bottle guide, stop, sensor timing, label-head datum | Mark a reference line on the bottle and compare label position over a short run. |
| Position drifts or varies between bottles | Bottle rotation/spacing, web tracking, tension, sensor repeatability | Watch whether the bottle or label web moves inconsistently before touching timing. |
| A wrinkle begins at the leading edge or seam | Label release, first contact, wrap geometry, label shape | Slow the process safely and observe where the fold first appears. |
| A bubble appears under a flat area | Surface cleanliness/moisture, contact pressure, trapped air | Inspect bottle and label condition; apply a sample by hand with smooth, progressive pressure. |
| A corner or edge lifts after application | Surface condition, adhesive/substrate match, dwell or application conditions | Check whether the defect is present immediately or develops later; consult label technical data. |

## Keep a Defect Log

Record bottle lot, label roll, machine setting, speed, defect type, and sample number. Photograph the same area and angle. A quick log helps distinguish a change that affected every bottle from random variation. Do not alter roll tension, sensor position, bottle guides, and pressure together; if the result changes, you will not know which adjustment mattered.

## Find the First Abnormal Step

Observe one container through the applicator rather than inspecting only the finished bottle. Note when the defect first becomes visible. Stop safely before reaching into moving equipment. For powered machines, use the manufacturer's operating and lockout instructions when adjustments require access near moving parts.

| If the fault begins… | Check in this order |
| --- | --- |
| Before the label leaves the backing | Roll orientation and threading; label gap or mark detection; liner path and take-up; side guides; damage or inconsistent spacing in the roll. Confirm against the machine manual. |
| At the peel edge or first contact | Peel-plate alignment and edge condition; label position; sensor timing; bottle arrival and stability. A repeatable early/late label suggests a registration or timing issue, but verify the product sensor and bottle spacing first. |
| During wrap or wipe-down | Bottle support and rotation; pressure roller/brush contact; guide alignment; label length and shape; whether the bottle is tapered, seamed, wet, dusty, or textured. Change contact pressure gradually. |
| Only after the bottle leaves | Adhesion and surface compatibility; condensation or contamination; application temperature and dwell conditions; handling before adhesive has bonded. Compare with the label supplier's technical data sheet. |

## Troubleshoot Crooked or Spiraling Labels

Check whether the bottle is stable and square to the label head. On a tabletop machine, bottle supports and guides must hold the container without preventing the rotation the machine needs. On an automatic line, observe bottle spacing, conveyor motion, and any wrap belt or roller. If the label web itself wanders, inspect the roll path and tension before correcting application timing.

If every label rotates by a similar amount, verify the bottle datum and head alignment. If the angle changes from bottle to bottle, look for inconsistent bottle motion or web tracking. A pressure or speed change may mask the symptom while leaving the cause in place.

## Troubleshoot Wrinkles and Folds

Identify where the fold begins. A fold starting at the leading edge can point toward early contact, a skewed label path, or bottle motion. A wrinkle that forms as the label wraps may involve label length or shape, surface contour, pressure, or a mismatch between label feed and bottle rotation. Confirm one cause at a time with a short run.

Inspect the label construction and bottle surface as well as the machine. Stiff face material, a seam, a compound curve, roughness, oil, dust, or moisture can change how the label lays down. Do not assume more pressure is always better; excessive or uneven contact can create a different defect or damage the package.

## Troubleshoot Bubbles and Lifting Edges

For bubbles, check for trapped air, a rough or contaminated surface, and uneven application pressure. Avery Dennison's troubleshooting guide lists surface condition, moisture, application conditions, and inconsistent pressure among possible causes, and recommends cleaning the substrate and using sufficient, consistent massage or squeegee pressure. Follow the exact label product's technical data sheet; that supplier guidance is not a universal setting for every label stock.

For lifting edges, first note when lifting appears. Immediate lift and lift after storage may have different causes. Check bottle cleanliness and dryness, label alignment on curved areas, adhesive compatibility with the actual substrate, and application conditions. Ask the label supplier to confirm suitability for the bottle resin, coatings, temperature, and intended environment. A labeling machine cannot correct an adhesive that is unsuitable for the surface.

## A Controlled Adjustment Sequence

- **Establish a baseline.** Use one bottle lot and one label roll; record current settings and keep a small, pre-agreed baseline sample.
- **Correct the obvious physical setup.** Confirm the roll path, label-head alignment, bottle guide/support, and clean contact surfaces against the machine manual.
- **Change one variable.** Adjust only the likely cause, in a small documented step. Do not start by changing speed and pressure together.
- **Run a short repeatable sample.** Compare position, defect count, and first point of failure with the baseline.
- **Confirm the fix at normal operating conditions.** A low-speed sample can help isolate a fault, but final acceptance must reflect the intended production rate and agreed quality limits.
- **Record the approved setup.** Save settings, roll details, bottle format, photos, and any operator instructions so the result can be repeated.

## When to Stop Adjusting

If defects continue after the bottle and label path are stable, stop compensating with repeated timing changes. Send the machine supplier a short video showing the roll, peel point, bottle entry, and finished defect; include bottle dimensions, label construction, speed, and a few labeled samples. Ask the label converter to check adhesive/substrate compatibility when edges lift or bubbles develop after application. If the actual package format is outside the machine's stated range, request a sample test or a different application configuration.
