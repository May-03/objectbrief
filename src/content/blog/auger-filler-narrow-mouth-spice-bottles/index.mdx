> **Quick answer.** A narrow-mouth bottle can be filled with a screw auger only when the whole dosing path works with the actual spice and package. A small nozzle that fits through the opening is not enough. Before buying, send the supplier representative product and bottle samples, then evaluate dose variation, nozzle clearance, powder left on the rim, bottle stability, closure fit and accepted output in one documented trial. If the seller cannot run the real combination, treat the machine as unqualified for that package.

This guide covers one production task: filling dry spice or seasoning blends into narrow-neck rigid bottles. It helps small producers decide whether a bench or semi-automatic auger unit can perform that step reliably enough to justify a purchase.

## Start With the Bottle Opening, Not the Machine Label

"Neck size" is often ambiguous: a drawing may list finish or thread dimensions, while the clear bore is smaller. A shaker insert, reducer, liner or seal may further reduce usable space. Request the package drawing and measure the minimum bore at the throat on actual bottles. Record neck height, shoulder location, bottle height and all parts installed after filling.

The question for the filler is not simply whether its outlet diameter is less than the opening. The complete nozzle must approach and withdraw without striking the rim, threads or bottle shoulder; it must stay centered while the container is presented; and the dose must land below the area that needs to stay clean for closing. A flexible operator holding the bottle by hand may manage a fit that becomes unreliable in a guide rail or fixture. The test should use the presentation method planned for production.

Do not treat a supplier's advertised neck threshold as a general engineering limit. A competitor guide, for example, discusses openings below 20 mm and its own machine options, but that is a vendor-specific selection claim, not a universal cutoff for all spices and bottle designs. [3] A 20 mm bore may be easy for one combination and unusable for another because nozzle geometry, bottle tolerance, powder behavior and closing method differ.

Send at least the bottle drawing and samples with all parts that affect filling or closure. Ask the supplier to identify the exact nozzle, auger, hopper arrangement and bottle fixture used in the test. If it proposes a different nozzle or a custom guide, have the seller confirm that the quoted configuration includes it and that it can be cleaned and changed over as needed.

## Describe the Powder and the Dose in Production Terms

"Spice powder" is not a sufficiently precise trial material description. A finely milled chili blend, coarse pepper, dehydrated garlic and a blended seasoning with salt or sugar can behave differently in the same hopper. Particle size, bulk density, moisture condition, cohesion and the proportions of each ingredient affect how material enters and travels through a screw. The appearance of the powder in a sample jar does not establish its behavior after transport, storage or a long production run.

Research supports testing material and process together. A peer-reviewed study of ten powders examined material attributes and auger settings against dosing quality, but focused on pharmaceutical powders and does not certify spice-bottle performance. An experimental comparison of screw and vibratory feeders likewise found feeder performance depended on powder flow properties. [1] [2] Ask for a trial with your actual blend.

Define the target as net product mass per bottle and specify what counts as acceptable. A volume mark or "one scoop" is not a substitute for a target mass if the product is sold by weight. Record the method used to determine net contents, such as weighing the empty container and subtracting tare from each filled sample. State any customer, label or internal acceptance band that applies to your product. Do not ask the supplier to choose a tolerance for you.

Also define usable headspace. An acceptable dose can still obstruct a shaker insert, contaminate the closure, or look overfilled after the powder settles. Check the fill level immediately after dosing and again after the settling or handling step that occurs in your own process. If the bulk density changes between lots or blends, the same mass may occupy a different volume. That is a packaging-fit question to resolve before fixing the machine recipe.

## Make the Nozzle and Powder Path Prove Themselves

A trial needs to reveal more than whether powder comes out. Observe the nozzle as it aligns with the opening, dispenses and retracts. Check for contact marks, powder caught on the threads, bridging across the mouth, product clinging to the outlet or a plume that settles on the rim. If the nozzle enters the neck, record how far it travels and whether insertion depth can be set consistently without touching the bottle.

A tight neck can make air and powder movement harder to observe. The supplier should test whether displaced air can escape without pushing powder back toward the mouth, and whether fill speed or nozzle position changes that result. Commercial equipment guides identify bottle presentation, nozzle clearance, dust, fill height and the move to capping as linked design questions. [4] They are useful as a checklist, but their general advice is not evidence that a particular filler will pass your trial.

The closure is part of the fill test. Fit the actual sifter or shaker insert, cap, liner and seal after dosing. Then inspect the contact surfaces and threads for loose powder, and check that the closure seats as intended. If your package uses an induction seal, have the supplier test the intended sealing route or clearly separate the filling test from the later seal validation. A clean-looking bottle exterior alone does not prove that the neck finish is ready for closing.

Test the planned bottle-holding method too. A tall, light bottle may shift during hand loading or behave differently in a guide or fixture. Repeat placements using the actual package; note holder requirements, changeover effort, visibility and safe operator access.

## Run a Short Acceptance Trial With Written Criteria

Agree on the test before samples are sent. Record the product lot, bottle and closure versions, target net weight, machine configuration, settings, operator method and date. Ask for a video that shows setup and a continuous sequence of fills, not only selected finished bottles. Request the measured results in a simple table so that the trial can be repeated after delivery.

| Trial item | What to record |
| --- | --- |
| Material and package | Spice blend and lot; bottle drawing/version; measured minimum clear bore; cap, insert, liner or seal components. |
| Machine setup | Model and serial/configuration if assigned; auger and nozzle identifiers; hopper or agitator arrangement; settings and adjustments. |
| Dose results | Individual net weight for each sampled bottle, tare method, sample timing, mean and spread; note start-up and later fills separately. |
| Bottle interface | Nozzle alignment and insertion depth; contact, bridging, blowback or visible residue; bottle movement and fixture used. |
| Finished package | Headspace immediately and after settling; rim/thread condition; insert and cap fit; sealing result if included in the test. |
| Production pace | Accepted bottles per hour including loading, weighing, adjustments, normal pauses and closure handling; identify excluded steps. |

Do not accept an average alone. A mean near the target can hide individual bottles that are too light or heavy. Inspect the individual readings against your predetermined acceptance band, and agree how rejected, purged or reworked bottles count. If start-up doses differ from steady operation, preserve that distinction in the report. Avoid declaring a universal sample size from a short demonstration; choose a sample plan that is appropriate to your risk and production volume.

Repeat the test after a stop and restart or normal hopper refill. If blend or supplier changes are expected, include a representative lot with more difficult flow characteristics. The goal is to check whether the quoted setup handles the range you actually expect.

Measure throughput as accepted, closed-ready bottles per operator hour, not the filler's nominal cycle rate. Include bottle loading, tare checks, adjustments, wiping or cleaning and handoff to the next operation. A fast burst of dispensing may not help if the operator must manually correct doses or clean every rim. Conversely, a lower nominal rate may be sufficient for a small producer if it consistently yields saleable bottles and fits the available labor.

## Use Trial Symptoms to Choose the Next Test

| Observed result | Likely question | Next action |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Nozzle touches the lip or thread | Is the measured clear bore smaller or less centered than assumed? | Recheck samples and drawing; request a different outlet or guide and repeat the full approach/retract cycle. |
| Powder appears on rim or closure threads | Does the dose plume, nozzle position or bottle presentation leave the sealing area dirty? | Inspect at slow and target settings; test the revised nozzle height/position and then fit the closure. |
| Weight spread changes during the run | Is the blend feeding consistently as the hopper level, refill state or settings change? | Compare individual weights by run segment; test the same batch under recorded settings and ask for a feed-path adjustment trial. |
| Fill height is too close to the shoulder | Does the target mass occupy more volume than the bottle can accept below its closure parts? | Measure settled fill height and headspace; compare a suitable package or confirm the actual bulk density range. |
| Bottle shifts or tips | Is manual holding or the proposed guide stable at the intended pace? | Trial the production fixture and repeated loading; include fixture cost and cleaning in the quote. |
| Average weight passes but some bottles fail | Does the data hide outliers or start-up variation? | Request full individual readings and agree an acceptance method before the next run. |

A symptom is a prompt for investigation, not a diagnosis. For example, powder on the rim could come from nozzle position, an unsettled dose, handling or a closure part that sheds material. Ask the supplier to change one relevant setting at a time and show the result. That makes the cause easier to identify and gives operators a reproducible setup rather than a collection of undocumented adjustments.

## When to Keep the Narrow-Mouth Bottle

Keep the current bottle if the machine can dose within the agreed weight criteria, leave the closure area clean, present bottles safely and meet the required accepted output. Compare that route with a wider-mouth bottle when clearance or cleaning remains unreliable. A package change can simplify filling, but it may alter shelf presentation, label area, consumer use, shipping protection or compatibility with existing caps and retail requirements. Evaluate those effects with the packaging owner rather than treating a wider opening as a free fix.

If the supplier cannot pass the product-and-package test, consider a different nozzle and holder, a different dosing technology, or a revised container. Each option changes the trial. A weigh-based filler may be preferable when mass control dominates, but its fit, cycle time, cleaning and cost also need evidence. Do not conclude that an auger is the right machine merely because the listing says "spice" or "powder bottle filler."

Ask for the exact configuration and trial conditions in the quotation. "Suitable for small bottles" is not a measurable specification. The supplier should state the tested opening, target fill, tested blend, nozzle and auger used, observed results and any parts excluded from the price. If results are conditional on manual bottle positioning or an optional fixture, write that down before comparing offers.

## Send a Trial-Ready Request for Quotation

A useful RFQ gives each supplier the same information and asks for evidence in return. Attach the bottle drawing and representative samples; identify the closure pieces and the product lot. Then request a written response to these points:

- Can you run this exact spice blend and bottle together, and return individual net-weight readings rather than only a stated accuracy?
- What are the clear nozzle dimensions, insertion depth, approach/retract method and bottle-centering arrangement in the quoted configuration?
- What trial settings and configuration produced the result, and can you provide a continuous test video?
- How were rim cleanliness, headspace, closure fit and any seal step inspected?
- What accepted output did you observe with operator loading and normal weighing or adjustment included?
- Which product-contact parts, cleaning steps, changeover parts, guards, electrical requirements, warranty and spare parts are included?
- Which result remains untested or depends on a custom component, and what would that change in price or lead time?

Keep these answers with the sample report and order specification as a commissioning baseline. If the seller retests after a configuration change, ask for revised settings and individual results; an earlier test does not prove the new setup.
